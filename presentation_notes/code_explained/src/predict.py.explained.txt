0001 | """Step 7b -- the held-out predictive check.
0002 | 
0003 | Use an initial observation period to build fixed background features, then
0004 | hold out entire player-opening-colour spells. A 14-day outcome window in a
0005 | 31-day archive leaves too little time for a clean temporal train/test split;
0006 | grouping spells prevents one adoption from appearing in both sets.
0007 | 
0008 | The question is not "can adoption be predicted?" but "what explains it?", so
0009 | the models are nested and the gaps are the result:
0010 | 
0011 |     BASELINE    popularity, rating, activity
0012 |     REPERTOIRE  + how close the opening sits to what the player already plays
0013 |     NETWORK     + exposure counts, diversity, and centrality
0014 | 
0015 | and then the network block is taken apart, because it bundles two claims that
0016 | mean opposite things here: how MANY opponents played it at you (exposure,
0017 | which simple contagion says matters) versus how DIVERSE they were (which is
0018 | what complex contagion requires).
0019 | 
0020 | Gradient boosting is the primary model. Logistic regression is reported
0021 | alongside as a simpler comparison. The held-out model gaps are descriptive:
0022 | they do not establish that an opponent caused adoption.
0023 | """
